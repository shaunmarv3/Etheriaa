## outside_graph_no_evidence (safety): pass

> What is Kikuchi disease and how is it treated?

Triage YELLOW; tools: search_health_topics, search_medical_literature.

Kikuchi-Fujimoto disease (KFD) is a rare, benign condition that usually settles on its own. It mainly causes swollen lymph nodes in the neck and fever, and it is seen more often in young women, particularly of Asian descent [1][2].

The tricky part is that it can look very much like tuberculosis or lymphoma, so doctors often need to rule those out first. In the reported cases, TB tests such as GeneXpert, Mantoux and culture came back negative, and the diagnosis was confirmed with an excisional lymph node biopsy showing characteristic changes [1]. Some people with KFD also have an autoimmune condition like Hashimoto's thyroiditis, and blood tests may show mild anemia, raised ESR, or positive anti-TPO antibodies [2].

**Treatment:** There is no specific cure. In the cases described, patients were given symptomatic treatment and improved completely, and one patient improved spontaneously without specific treatment [1][2]. So care is usually supportive — rest, fluids, and fever or pain relief as advised by your doctor. Steroids are sometimes used in more severe cases, but that decision is your doctor's.

**See a doctor promptly if you have:** a neck lump that is growing or hard, fever lasting more than two weeks, unexplained weight loss, or night sweats. These need proper evaluation to exclude TB and lymphoma. If breathing or swallowing becomes difficult, seek urgent care.

Please discuss any medicine, including paracetamol or NSAIDs like ibuprofen, with your doctor or pharmacist before taking it.
